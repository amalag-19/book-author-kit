How it works

Open a folder, say one sentence, start writing.

Create the book

Open the kit in Cursor and type "Create my book." The assistant installs the tools, creates your book's folder, and checks that it builds. No terminal, no admin password.

Write the playbook first

Say "Set up my book." The assistant interviews you about your reader, angle, voice and chapter structure, and writes your answers into the book's playbook. It never invents your experience.

Draft, check, publish

Talk each chapter through, and the AI drafts it from your words, then audits the draft against your own rules. One command builds the paperback and Kindle files. Preflight tells you when the book is safe to upload.

Preflight

Twenty checks stand between you and a bad upload.

Amazon will happily print a broken book. ./book preflight won't let you upload one. It prints either a list of blockers or "Cleared to upload."

  • Page count, trim size and KDP margins on every page
  • Every font embedded
  • ISBN present and its check digit valid
  • Cover spine width matches the final page count
  • Kindle cover resolution and ratio
  • EPUB structure, image alt text and rendered equations
  • Every figure referenced; captions match the prose
  • Diagram labels legible at print size
  • Back-cover type size, contrast and barcode zone
  • No [GAP], [FILL IN] or TODO left in the manuscript
  • Upload files identical to the latest build
  • Nothing built from a stale source

FAQ

Questions

Will it work for my subject? It was built for an ML book.

Yes. The playbooks contain no ML content. They're a method for any technical or expert nonfiction book, and the setup interview tailors them to your reader, your chapter structure and your voice. The ML book appears only as labelled examples showing what finished work looks like. The build handles what technical books have in common: code, tables, diagrams, equations and footnotes. It ships with a few callout box styles (questions, pitfalls); the assistant can add your own types, such as "Try it" or "Warning", in a few minutes.

Does the AI write my book?

No. The rules tell the assistant to draft only from your own material (talk-through transcripts and notes) and to mark anything missing as [GAP] instead of inventing it. Preflight refuses to clear a manuscript with gaps left in it.

Do I need to know LaTeX, pandoc or Python?

No. The setup installs them for you in a private folder, and the assistant runs the commands. You write in Markdown, which is plain text with a few symbols for headings and emphasis.

What do I need?

A Mac or Linux computer (Windows works through WSL) and Cursor. The first setup downloads about 150 MB, uses about 600 MB of disk, and takes up to fifteen minutes on a slow connection.

Can I use other AI assistants?

The kit is set up for Cursor. It also includes AGENTS.md and CLAUDE.md, so other coding assistants can follow the same rules, but the one-sentence setup is tested in Cursor.

What about platform rules and prices that change?

Amazon, LinkedIn and Substack figures that can change are marked with the date they were last checked, and the assistant is told to confirm them before relying on them.
